Topic 37: Reading Comprehension

Find main ideas, understand context, make inferences, and answer with evidence.

1. Four important reading skills

Skimming

Read quickly for the general idea.

Look at titles, headings, and first sentences.

Scanning

Search for a specific detail.

Look for dates, names, numbers, and key terms.

Inference

Combine clues and reasoning.

A good inference is supported, not invented.

Summarizing

State the main points briefly.

Use your own words and omit minor details.

2. Topic vs. main idea

Topic: what the passage is about.

Main idea: what the writer says about that topic.

Passage: “Walking to work can save money, add daily exercise, and reduce traffic congestion.”
Topic: Walking to work
Main idea: Walking to work has several practical benefits.

3. Use context clues

“Mina was reluctant to speak, so she waited until someone asked her a direct question.”

The context suggests reluctant means hesitant or unwilling.

Try the surrounding sentence first, then confirm the word in a dictionary if needed.

4. Signal words

Contrast: however, although, whereas
Cause/result: because, so, therefore
Example: for instance, such as
Sequence: first, next, finally

Reading Lab

Read the passage and answer the questions. Look for evidence first.

Reading strategy: a 5-step method

  1. Preview: read the title and headings.
  2. Ask: what is the passage likely to explain?
  3. Read: notice the main idea and linking words.
  4. Answer: find the line or clue supporting each response.
  5. Check: remove options that add unsupported information.
