Advanced Prepositions & Phrasal Verbs

Learn how small words change meaning, choose the right preposition in real sentences, and use common phrasal verbs naturally. Read the examples, try the activities, then test yourself with 40 questions.

1. What is a preposition?

A preposition connects a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase to another part of the sentence. It can show time, place, movement, method, cause, and other relationships.

Time

We meet at six. The exam is on Monday. I was born in 2003.

Place

The keys are under the desk. She is at work.

Movement

He walked into the room and ran across the road.

Method / agent

I sent it by email. The poem was written by Maya.

Preposition or adverb particle?

In look after the child, “after” is part of a verb expression and takes an object. In come in, “in” can act as an adverb particle with no object. Grammar labels can vary, so focus first on meaning and correct word order.

Preposition: She walked through the tunnel.
Phrasal verb: Please turn off the light.

A quick rule to remember

  • Learn a preposition together with its phrase: interested in, good at.
  • Learn phrasal verbs as vocabulary chunks: give up = stop trying.
  • Do not translate every phrase word-for-word from your first language.
  • There are exceptions: use these patterns as reliable guides, not as rules without exceptions.

2. Advanced prepositions of time

PrepositionUseExample
atExact time; some fixed phrasesat 7:30, at noon, at night
onDays and dateson Friday, on 13 October
inMonths, years, seasons, longer periodsin October, in 2026, in winter
byDeadline: no later thanSubmit it by Friday.
until / tillSomething continues up to a timeWe waited until 8 p.m.
withinBefore a period endsReply within two days.
duringAt some point in a period; followed by a nounduring the meeting
forDurationfor three hours
sinceStarting pointsince Monday / since 2020
Compare: I will finish by 5 (deadline). I will work until 5 (continue working up to 5). I will finish it within two hours (before two hours have passed).

Use “during + noun” (during class), but “while + subject + verb” (while I was in class).

4. Phrasal verbs

A phrasal verb combines a verb with a particle (and sometimes a preposition) to create a meaning that may differ from the original verb.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
give upstop trying / quitNever give up on your goals.
look aftertake care ofShe looks after her younger brother.
run intomeet unexpectedly; also collide withI ran into an old friend.
put offpostponeThey put off the meeting.
carry oncontinuePlease carry on working.
find outdiscover / learnWe found out the truth.
look forward tofeel happy about a future eventI look forward to meeting you.
get overrecover fromHe got over his cold.
turn downreject; reduce volume/heatShe turned down the offer.
take offremove; an aircraft leaves the groundPlease take off your shoes.
look upsearch for informationLook up the word in a dictionary.

Word order: separable and inseparable

Separable: turn down

Turn down the music. / Turn the music down.

With a pronoun: Turn it down.

Not: Turn down it.

Inseparable: look after

She looks after her brother.

She looks after him.

Not: She looks him after.

Verb + preposition: look forward to

“To” is a preposition here, so use a noun or -ing form.

I look forward to seeing you.

Not: look forward to see you.

Phrasal-verb grammar depends on the expression. Learn each verb’s meaning and whether its object can move.
