Tenses — Part 1

Understand five essential tenses: Simple Present, Present Continuous, Simple Past, Past Continuous, and Simple Future. Compare time, sentence patterns, and examples, then practise with 45 questions.

What is tense?

Tense helps show when an action or situation happens: now, before now, or after now. In this lesson, learn five common forms.

Present Simple PresentI walk to school every day.
Present Present ContinuousI am walking to school now.
Past Simple PastI walked to school yesterday.
Past Past ContinuousI was walking when it rained.
Future Simple FutureI will walk tomorrow.
Quick clue: Words such as every day, usually often suggest a routine; now, at the moment often suggest an action in progress; yesterday, last week suggest the past; tomorrow suggests the future. These are clues, not absolute rules.

Five tense patterns at a glance

Simple PresentSubject + base verb (add -s/-es for he/she/it)
She works.
Present ContinuousSubject + am/is/are + verb-ing
She is working.
Simple PastSubject + past form
She worked / She went.
Past ContinuousSubject + was/were + verb-ing
She was working.
Simple FutureSubject + will + base verb
She will work.

1. Simple Present

Use it for habits, routines, general truths, and facts.

I drink tea every morning. / The sun rises in the east. / She works in a bank.
Positive: I/you/we/they work; he/she/it works.
Negative: I do not work; she does not work.
Question: Do you work? Does she work?

2. Present Continuous

Use it for an action happening now or around this time.

I am reading now. / He is cooking dinner. / They are studying for exams.
Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
Negative: She is not sleeping.
Question: Are they coming?

Some verbs describing states, such as know, believe, and own, are not usually used in continuous forms in ordinary meanings: “I know the answer,” not usually “I am knowing the answer.”

Compare the present forms

RoutineHe plays football on Sundays.
Happening nowHe is playing football now.

Both sentences are about the present, but the first describes a habit and the second describes an action in progress.

3. Simple Past

Use it for a finished action or event in the past.

I visited my aunt yesterday. / She went to the market. / They watched a film last night.
Regular verbs often add -ed: walk → walked; clean → cleaned.
Irregular verbs change form: go → went; eat → ate; see → saw.
Negative: did not + base verb (did not go).
Question: Did + subject + base verb? (Did you go?)

4. Past Continuous

Use it for an action that was in progress at a time in the past. It often provides background or is interrupted by another action.

At 8 pm, I was studying. / They were sleeping when the phone rang.
Subject + was/were + verb-ing
I/he/she/it was working. You/we/they were working.
Negative: He was not listening. Question: Were you waiting?

Simple Past vs Past Continuous

“I cooked dinner” describes a completed past action. “I was cooking dinner when she arrived” describes an action in progress when another event happened.

5. Simple Future with “will”

Use will + base verb for predictions, decisions made at the moment of speaking, offers, promises, and future facts.

It will rain tomorrow. / I will help you. / I think our team will win.
Positive: She will come.
Negative: She will not (won't) come.
Question: Will she come?
After will, use the base verb: “will go,” not “will goes.”

Going to is also common for plans and predictions based on present evidence: “I am going to study tonight.” We will study more future forms later.
