The big idea: perfect vs continuous

Perfect = connection or completionConnects an action to a reference time. “I have finished my work.” The result matters now.
Continuous = in progress or durationFocuses on an action continuing over time. “I have been studying for two hours.”
A useful pattern: perfect uses have/has/had/will have + past participle (V3). perfect continuous uses have/has/had/will have + been + verb-ing.

Six key tense forms

Present PerfectI have eaten.
Present Perfect ContinuousI have been eating.
Past PerfectI had eaten.
Past Perfect ContinuousI had been eating.
Future ContinuousI will be eating.
Future PerfectI will have eaten.
Future Perfect ContinuousI will have been eating.

There are seven forms here because Future Continuous is also included as a key future form.

Ask two questions: When is the reference time? and Do I focus on the result or on duration/in-progress action?

1. Present Perfect

Form: subject + have/has + V3

PositiveI have finished. / She has finished.
NegativeI have not finished. / She has not finished.
QuestionHave you finished? / Has she finished?

When do we use it?

  • Life experience: “I have visited Delhi.”
  • A past action with a present result: “She has lost her keys.” (She does not have them now.)
  • A period that is not finished: “I have read two chapters today.”
Common words: already, just, yet, ever, never. “Yet” is common in questions and negatives: “Have you eaten yet?” “I haven't eaten yet.”

2. Present Perfect Continuous

Form: subject + have/has + been + verb-ing

Duration until nowI have been studying for two hours.
Recent activity with evidenceShe is tired because she has been running.
Compare: “I have read the book” emphasizes completion/result. “I have been reading the book” emphasizes the activity or duration; it may not be finished.

3. Past Perfect

Form: subject + had + V3

Use it for an action completed before another past time or action.

When I arrived, the film had started.

The film started first. I arrived later.

PositiveThey had left before noon.
NegativeThey had not left before noon.
QuestionHad they left before noon?

4. Past Perfect Continuous

Form: subject + had + been + verb-ing

Focuses on duration or ongoing activity before a past point.

She had been waiting for an hour when the bus arrived.
Past perfect = earlier completed event. Past perfect continuous = activity/duration before a past event. “He was tired because he had been working all night.”

5. Future Continuous

Form: will + be + verb-ing

An action will be in progress at a particular future time.

At 8 pm tomorrow, I will be studying.

6. Future Perfect

Form: will + have + V3

An action will be completed before a future deadline.

By Friday, she will have finished the project.

7. Future Perfect Continuous

Form: will + have + been + verb-ing

Focuses on how long an activity will have continued by a future point.

By June, I will have been working here for two years.
Memory trick: by + future time often appears with future perfect. For + duration by a future point often appears with future perfect continuous.
