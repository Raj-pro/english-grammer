Topic 35: Pronunciation, Word Stress & Intonation

Practise clear speech, syllables, sentence stress, and natural pitch movement.

1. Syllables and word stress

A syllable is a beat or sound unit in a word. Word stress means one syllable stands out more than the others.

TA-ble — first syllable
be-GIN — second syllable
in-for-MA-tion — third syllable
HO-tel — second syllable

Capital letters show the stressed syllable. Dictionaries often mark stress with a symbol such as /əˈraɪv/ for “arrive.”

2. Sentence stress

Important content words—often nouns, main verbs, adjectives, and adverbs—usually receive more stress. Small grammar words may be reduced.

I wanted the BLUE shirt, not the red one.
I'll meet you TOMORROW.
Yes, I CAN do it.

Changing the stressed word can change the focus or contrast.

3. Intonation: the movement of pitch

Falling pitch ↘

“I finished the report. ↘”

Often sounds complete or definite.

Rising pitch ↗

“Are you ready? ↗”

Often used in yes/no questions or checks.

Pitch depends on context

“You finished already? ↗”

May express surprise or ask for confirmation.

These are common patterns, not rigid rules. Intonation varies by speaker, accent, and situation.

4. Clear pronunciation

  • Practise sound contrasts with minimal pairs: ship / sheep, cap / cab.
  • Keep final sounds audible when needed: map, maps; cap, cab.
  • Speak in meaningful thought groups instead of pausing after every word.
  • Listen, imitate, record, compare, and repeat.
  • Aim for understandable speech, not for erasing your accent.
When I got home / I made some tea.

Practice Lab

Select a drill, read the model aloud, and practise three times.

Five-minute speaking routine

  1. Listen (1 minute): choose a short English sentence from a lesson or recording.
  2. Mark (1 minute): underline the important words and mark pauses with /.
  3. Imitate (1 minute): repeat the sentence while copying its rhythm and pitch.
  4. Record (1 minute): say it naturally and listen back if recording is available.
  5. Repeat (1 minute): focus on one improvement, not everything at once.

Try this sentence

“I wanted to call you yesterday, but I got home late.”

Possible thought groups: “I wanted to call you yesterday / but I got home late.” Emphasize the words that carry your intended meaning.
