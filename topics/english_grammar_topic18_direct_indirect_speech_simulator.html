Direct and Indirect Speech

Learn how to quote someone's exact words and report what they said.

What are direct and indirect speech?

Direct speech repeats the speaker's exact words, usually inside quotation marks.

Indirect speech (reported speech) tells us what someone said without quoting the exact words.

DIRECT

Riya said, “I am tired.”

INDIRECT

Riya said that she was tired.

When changing to indirect speech, check the reporting verb, pronouns, tense, time/place words, and sentence type.

Reporting verbs

Say focuses on the words: “She said that she was ready.”

Tell normally needs a person as an object: “She told me that she was ready.”

Say: “He said to me, ‘Wait.’” → He told me to wait. Avoid “He told that…” without an object in standard reported speech.

Reporting statements

A common pattern is subject + said (that) + reported clause. “That” is often optional.

Direct: Amir said, “I like tea.”
Indirect: Amir said (that) he liked tea.
Direct: Sara said, “I have finished my work.”
Indirect: Sara said (that) she had finished her work.
Direct: They said, “We will call you.”
Indirect: They said (that) they would call me.

Typical tense backshift

Direct speechReported speech (common change)
Simple present: “I work.”Simple past: he said he worked.
Present continuous: “I am working.”Past continuous: he said he was working.
Simple past: “I worked.”Past perfect: he said he had worked.
Present perfect: “I have worked.”Past perfect: he said he had worked.
Will: “I will go.”Would: he said he would go.
Can: “I can swim.”Could: he said he could swim.

Backshift is common when the reporting verb is in the past, but it is not automatic in every context. If something is still true or the reporting context makes it relevant, the original tense may be retained.

Questions in reported speech

Use statement word order, not question word order. Usually remove the question mark from the reported clause.

Direct: He asked, “Where do you live?”
Indirect: He asked me where I lived.
Direct: She asked, “Are you ready?”
Indirect: She asked me if/whether I was ready.

For yes/no questions, use if or whether. For wh-questions, keep the question word (what, where, why, when, who, how).

Commands and requests

Direct: Dad said, “Close the door.”
Indirect: Dad told me to close the door.
Direct: She said, “Please help me.”
Indirect: She asked me to help her.
Direct: The guard said, “Don't enter.”
Indirect: The guard warned us not to enter.

Pronoun, time and place changes

Change words according to who is reporting, when, and where. These are common changes—not fixed rules for every situation.

DirectOften becomes
I / wehe, she / they (depending on speaker)
my / ourhis, her / their
this / thesethat / those (if the context shifts)
nowthen
todaythat day
yesterdaythe day before
tomorrowthe next day / the following day

Example: On Monday, Dev said, “I will come here tomorrow.” → Dev said he would go there the next day. The exact wording depends on where and when the report is made.

Common mistakes

  • Incorrect: She asked where was I. Correct: She asked where I was.
  • Incorrect: He told that he was ill. Correct: He said that he was ill / He told me that he was ill.
  • Incorrect: She asked me to don't move. Correct: She told me not to move.
  • Do not backshift a universal truth unnecessarily: “The teacher said, ‘Water boils at 100°C at sea level.’” → The teacher said that water boils at 100°C at sea level.
