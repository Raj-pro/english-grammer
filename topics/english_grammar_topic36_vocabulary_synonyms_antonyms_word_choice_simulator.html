Topic 36: Vocabulary, Synonyms, Antonyms & Word Choice

Choose precise words, learn useful collocations, and make your English sound natural.

1. Synonyms and antonyms

Synonyms

Words with similar meanings.

begin ≈ start
reliable ≈ dependable
concise ≈ brief and clear

Antonyms

Words with opposite or contrasting meanings.

ancient ↔ modern
expand ↔ shrink
scarce ↔ abundant

Synonyms are not always interchangeable. Check the tone, context, and grammar around the word.

2. Choose words by context

Everyday: “Can you help me?”

More formal: “Could you assist me?”

Precise: “Please verify the figures before submitting the report.”

Concise: “Because it rained, we stayed home.”

Formal does not always mean better. Choose the clearest word that suits your audience.

3. Collocations: words that naturally go together

make a decision
pay attention
heavy rain
strong coffee
meet a deadline
gain experience
raise an issue
reach a conclusion

Learn useful word pairs as whole phrases, not only as individual words.

4. Word families and grammar

clear (adjective) → clearly (adverb) → clarity (noun)

efficient (adjective) → efficiently (adverb) → efficiency (noun)

decide (verb) → decision (noun)

Check what the sentence needs: a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb.

A simple 10-minute vocabulary routine

  1. Choose five useful words from a text or conversation.
  2. Write a simple definition and one synonym or antonym.
  3. Record one natural collocation for each word.
  4. Write one sentence connected to your own life.
  5. Review yesterday's words before adding new ones.
