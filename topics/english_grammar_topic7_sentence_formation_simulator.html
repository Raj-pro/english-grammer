Sentence Formation

Learn how to put words in the right order, build clear sentences, make negative sentences, and ask questions. Practise with a word-order game and 40 questions.

What makes a complete sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. It usually begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, question mark, or exclamation mark.

SubjectWho or what the sentence is about.
VerbThe action or state.
Object (sometimes)The person or thing affected by the action.

Basic word order: Subject + Verb + Object

Ravi reads books.

Who? Ravi • Does what? reads • Reads what? books

Not every sentence needs an object. “Birds fly.” is complete: Subject + Verb. “She is happy.” is also complete: Subject + Verb + description.

More examples

I drink water. • The baby sleeps. • My friends are helpful. • We study English every evening.

Common sentence patterns

Subject + VerbBirds fly. / Babies cry.
Subject + Verb + ObjectShe likes music. / I opened the door.
Subject + Be + DescriptionHe is kind. / The soup is hot.
Subject + Verb + Place/TimeThey live in Delhi. / I study at night.

Put the words in the correct order

English usually follows a predictable order. Start with who/what, then the verb, then the object or extra information.

Usually: Subject → Verb → Object → Place → Time
Example: “I read a book at home every night.” This order can change for emphasis, but it is a useful beginner pattern.
Example 1Words: “tea / drinks / Meena” → Meena drinks tea.
Example 2Words: “to school / walks / he” → He walks to school.
Example 3Words: “every day / English / we / practise” → We practise English every day.

Statements, negatives, and questions

Positive statementShe likes apples.
NegativeShe does not like apples.
Yes/no questionDoes she like apples?
Wh-questionWhat does she like?

Using be verbs

Statement: They are ready. → Negative: They are not ready. → Question: Are they ready?

Using action verbs in the present simple

Statement: He plays chess. → Negative: He does not play chess. → Question: Does he play chess?

Using action verbs in the past simple

Statement: She visited us. → Negative: She did not visit us. → Question: Did she visit us?

After does and did, use the base form: “Does he work?” and “Did she call?” not “Does he works?” or “Did she called?”

Sentence-building checklist

  1. Start with a subject.
  2. Choose a verb that matches the subject and tense.
  3. Add an object if the verb needs one.
  4. Add useful details about place, time, or manner.
  5. Begin with a capital letter and use correct punctuation.
  6. For questions, check the word order and helping verb.
