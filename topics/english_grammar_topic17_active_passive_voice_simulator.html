Active and Passive Voice

Learn who does an action, who receives it, and how to change sentence voice.

What is voice?

Active voice: the subject does the action.

Passive voice: the subject receives the action.

ACTIVE

The chef cooked the meal.

The chef performs the action.

PASSIVE

The meal was cooked (by the chef).

The meal receives the action.

Both sentences describe the same event, but they focus on different things.

Remember

To make a normal passive sentence, the active sentence usually needs an object: Someone opened the door. → The door was opened.

A sentence like “The baby slept” has no direct object, so it normally cannot be changed into a passive sentence.

The passive formula

Object + correct form of “be” + past participle (V3) + optional “by” + doer

Change the active object into the passive subject. Choose the correct form of be to match the tense, then use the past participle (V3) of the main verb.

Active
People grow rice.
Passive
Rice is grown (by people).
Active
They repaired the road.
Passive
The road was repaired.

Three conversion steps

  1. Find the object in the active sentence.
  2. Move the object to the beginning.
  3. Use the matching form of be + V3; add “by + doer” only if useful.

Example: “Maya writes the report.” → “The report is written by Maya.”

Pronouns may change: he → him after “by”; she → her; they → them; I → me; we → us.

Passive voice across tenses

Keep the original tense. The form of be changes; the main verb becomes V3.

Simple present: They make cars. → Cars are made.
Present continuous: They are repairing the road. → The road is being repaired.
Simple past: The chef cooked dinner. → Dinner was cooked.
Past continuous: They were building the bridge. → The bridge was being built.
Present perfect: They have completed the work. → The work has been completed.
Past perfect: They had finished the task. → The task had been finished.
Future: They will announce the results. → The results will be announced.
Modal: You must follow the rules. → The rules must be followed.

Perfect continuous passive forms are grammatically possible but often awkward, so they are usually avoided in everyday English.

When is passive voice useful?

Doer unknown
My bike was stolen.
Doer unimportant
The road is cleaned every day.
Focus on result
The application has been approved.
Formal/scientific style
The samples were tested at 20°C.

Should you include “by…”?

Use by + doer when the person or thing doing the action matters.

  • “The novel was written by Jane Austen.” — the writer matters.
  • “The window was broken.” — the doer may be unknown or irrelevant.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong: The cake was ate. Correct: The cake was eaten.
  • Wrong: The room cleaned yesterday. Correct: The room was cleaned yesterday.
  • Wrong: The letters is sent. Correct: The letters are sent.
  • Do not change the tense accidentally: “They will open it” → “It will be opened,” not “It was opened.”
