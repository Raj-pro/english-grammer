Professional English: Interviews, Meetings & Workplace Communication

Practise answering interview questions, writing clear work messages, giving project updates, and communicating politely in meetings.

1. The STAR method for interview answers

S — Situation

What was happening?

T — Task

What needed to be done?

A — Action

What did you personally do?

R — Result

What changed or improved?

Example: “Our deployment process was slow (Situation). I needed to make it more repeatable (Task). I helped automate the build and test steps (Action). The team could deploy more consistently (Result).” Use only details that are true for your experience.

2. Professional phrases you can reuse

Clarify

“Could you clarify the deadline?”

Update

“The main task is complete. I’m testing it now.”

Raise a concern

“One concern is the delivery timeline.”

Ask for time

“Could we discuss extending the deadline to Thursday?”

Offer help

“Would it help if I reviewed the draft?”

Confirm

“Just to confirm, I’ll send it by 3 p.m.”

3. Clear project updates

Use this four-part structure:

  1. Done: What have you completed?
  2. Now: What are you working on?
  3. Next: What will you do next?
  4. Blocker: Is anything stopping progress?
“The dashboard changes are complete. I’m testing them now. Next, I’ll share the results with the team. I’m currently waiting for access to the staging environment.”

4. Professional email structure

  1. Specific subject line
  2. Greeting
  3. Purpose in the first sentence
  4. Important context and clear request
  5. Polite closing and your name
Subject: Request for Project Status Update

Hi Priya,

Could you share the latest status of the testing work when you have a moment? We’re preparing the release plan for Friday.

Thank you,
Raj

5. Meeting habits

  • Review the agenda before the meeting.
  • Keep comments relevant and concise.
  • Disagree with ideas respectfully, not with personal attacks.
  • Write down decisions, action owners, and deadlines.
  • End by confirming the next steps.

Useful answer framework

For experience questions, use STAR. For general questions, try:

Point → Reason → Example → Link to role
“I enjoy solving practical problems. I like finding reliable ways to improve a process. For example, I worked on automating a repetitive task. That experience is relevant to this role because…”

15-minute professional English routine

5 min — Answer

Choose one interview question and write 3–5 key points.

5 min — Speak

Practise aloud, keeping a clear structure and natural pace.

5 min — Review

Check clarity, grammar, examples, and unnecessary repetition.

Weekly challenge: practise one interview answer, one work update, and one professional email each week.

Quick challenge: give a project update

Imagine you have completed a task, are testing a change, and are waiting for access to a system. Write a 3–4 sentence update that includes what is done, what is next, and the blocker.
