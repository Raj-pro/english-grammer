Questions and Question Tags

Learn how to ask clear questions in English: yes/no questions, WH-questions, subject and object questions, indirect questions, and short question tags.

Why do questions have different word order?

English often changes word order to make a question. With most main verbs, we use a helping verb such as do, does, or did. With be and modal verbs, we usually move that verb before the subject.

StatementYou like tea.
QuestionDo you like tea?
StatementShe is ready.
QuestionIs she ready?
Remember: “Do you like…?” but “Are you…?” Do not say “Do you are ready?”

Question types

Yes/no questionAre you tired? — Yes, I am.
WH-questionWhere do you live? — I live in Delhi.
Subject questionWho called you? — Ravi called me.
Object questionWho did you call? — I called Ravi.
Indirect questionCould you tell me where the station is?
Question tagYou are ready, aren't you?

Yes/no questions

Present simpleDo you work here?
Does she work here?
Past simpleDid they arrive?
Did he call?
Be verbAre you ready?
Was she happy?
Modal verbCan you swim?
Should we leave?
Present continuousAre they studying?
Perfect tenseHave you finished?
Had he left?
After do/does/did, use the base form: “Does she like it?” “Did they go?” Not “Does she likes?” or “Did they went?”

Short answers

Are you ready? — Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Does he work here? — Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.

Can you drive? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

WH-questions

WhoWho is at the door? (person)
WhatWhat is your name? (thing/information)
WhereWhere do you live? (place)
WhenWhen does class start? (time)
WhyWhy are you late? (reason)
WhichWhich colour do you prefer? (choice)
WhoseWhose bag is this? (ownership)
HowHow did you solve it? (method/manner)
How often / long / many / muchHow often do you exercise? How long will it take? How many books? How much water?
Subject vs. object questions: “Who called you?” asks who performed the action; no do/does/did is needed. “Who did you call?” asks who received the action; use did + subject + base verb.

Indirect questions

Direct: “Where is the station?”

Polite/indirect: “Could you tell me where the station is?”

In an indirect question, use statement word order inside the question: “Do you know where she lives?” not “Do you know where does she live?”

Question tags

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement. It often checks information or invites agreement.

Positive statement → negative tagYou are tired, aren't you?
Negative statement → positive tagShe isn't late, is she?
Present simpleHe works here, doesn't he?
Past simpleThey left early, didn't they?
Modal verbYou can swim, can't you?
Have/has perfectShe has finished, hasn't she?
Rule: opposite polarity + the matching auxiliary + pronoun. If the statement has no auxiliary in the present/past simple, use do/does/did.

Special forms

  • I'm late, aren't I? (common standard tag)
  • Let's go, shall we?
  • Open the door, will you? (request)
  • Everyone is here, aren't they? (“they” can refer to everyone.)
