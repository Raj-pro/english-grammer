Listening Comprehension

Practise catching the main idea, listening for details, understanding context, and checking what you heard.

1. Listen for gist

On your first listen, focus on the general situation: who is speaking, what they are discussing, and why.

If two people discuss a delayed train, the gist is the travel delay—not every exact word.

2. Listen for specific information

Before listening again, identify what you need to catch: a time, date, name, place, price, reason, or next step.

Question: “When is the appointment?”
Listen for: the day and time, including any correction.

3. Use context and signal words

  • Correction: actually, I mean, rather
  • Contrast: but, however, although
  • Reason: because, since
  • Sequence: first, next, finally
  • Approximation: about, roughly, around

If you miss one word, keep listening. The next sentence may explain it.

4. Ask for clarification

“Sorry, could you say that again?”
“Could you speak a little more slowly, please?”
“Just to confirm, did you say Tuesday at ten?”

Comprehension check

Summarize what you heard

A 10-minute listening routine

  1. First listen: understand the general topic without pausing.
  2. Second listen: answer two or three specific questions.
  3. Check: read the transcript, if available, and identify missed words.
  4. Repeat: listen again and say one or two sentences aloud.
  5. Review: write a one-sentence summary.
Important: Do not translate every word in your head. Focus on the message and use context to fill small gaps.
