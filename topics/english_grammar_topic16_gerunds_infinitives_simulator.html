Gerunds and Infinitives

Learn when to use verb + -ing and to + base verb, including common verb patterns, prepositions, and verbs whose meanings change.

Two common forms of verbs

Gerund = verb + -ing used like a nounReading helps me relax.
I enjoy reading.
Infinitive = to + base verbI want to read.
She came here to study.
The form often depends on the word before it. Learn common patterns rather than trying to guess every time.

Quick comparison

After certain verbsenjoy reading; avoid eating; finish working
After other verbswant to go; decide to stay; hope to win
After a prepositiongood at drawing; interested in learning; before leaving
PurposeI went to the shop to buy milk.
Use a gerund after a preposition: “She is interested in learning English.” Not “interested in to learn.”

When do we use gerunds?

As the subjectSwimming is good exercise.
After enjoyI enjoy cooking.
After avoidHe avoided answering.
After finishThey finished cleaning.
After mindWould you mind opening the window?
After suggestShe suggested taking a break.
After a prepositionHe left without saying goodbye.
Common patternskeep doing, practise doing, consider doing, miss doing, admit doing
Useful memory list: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, practise, consider, keep, miss, admit + gerund.

When do we use infinitives?

After wantI want to learn.
After decideThey decided to leave.
After hopeWe hope to win.
After planShe plans to travel.
After promiseHe promised to help.
After learnI learned to drive.
PurposeWe went out to buy food.
Adjective + infinitiveIt is easy to understand.
Useful memory list: want, decide, hope, plan, promise, agree, refuse, learn, need + to-infinitive.

Infinitive without “to”

After most modals: “can swim,” “should study,” “must go.” Also: “Let him go,” “make her laugh,” and “I saw him cross the road.”

Verbs that can take both forms

Like / love / hateI like reading. / I like to read.
Often similar in meaning.
Start / begin / continueIt started raining. / It started to rain.
Often similar in meaning.
Stop doingHe stopped smoking.
He quit the habit.
Stop to doHe stopped to smoke.
He paused another activity in order to smoke.
Remember doingI remember locking the door.
I have a memory of it.
Remember to doRemember to lock the door.
Do not forget the task.
Try doingTry restarting the phone.
Experiment with this method.
Try to doI tried to lift the box.
I made an effort; success is not guaranteed.
Forget doing / to doForget doing = no memory of a past event. Forget to do = fail to remember a task.
Regret doing / to doRegret doing = feel sorry about the past. Regret to say/inform = be sorry about what you are about to say.
The form can change the meaning, so read the whole sentence rather than memorising only the verb.
