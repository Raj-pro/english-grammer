Adverbs

Learn how adverbs add information about actions, descriptions, time, place, frequency, and degree. Explore word changes and sentence positions, then practise with 40 questions.

What is an adverb?

An adverb gives more information about a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or sometimes a whole sentence.

Describes a verbShe speaks softly.
Describes an adjectiveThe test was very easy.
Describes another adverbHe ran quite quickly.
Comments on a sentenceFortunately, nobody was hurt.
Compare: “She is a careful driver” (adjective describes driver) and “She drives carefully” (adverb describes drives).

Adjective or adverb?

AdjectiveThe music is loud.
AdverbThey played loudly.
AdjectiveHe is a fast runner.
AdverbHe runs fast.

Not all adverbs end in -ly. Fast, hard, late, early, well can be adverbs, depending on the sentence.

Six useful types of adverbs

1. Manner — how?carefully, slowly, happily
She writes carefully.
2. Time — when?now, yesterday, soon, already
We will leave soon.
3. Place — where?here, there, outside, nearby
Please wait outside.
4. Frequency — how often?always, usually, often, sometimes, never
He often walks.
5. Degree — how much?very, quite, too, almost, extremely
It is very cold.
6. Sentence adverbsfortunately, honestly, probably
Probably, they are right.

Questions adverbs answer

How did she speak? Clearly. When did he arrive? Yesterday. Where did they wait? Outside. How often do you exercise? Usually. To what degree is it difficult? Very.

How are adverbs formed?

Many adjectives + -lyquick → quickly
slow → slowly
careful → carefully
Adjectives ending in -yhappy → happily
easy → easily
Adjectives ending in -legentle → gently
simple → simply
Irregular or same formgood → well
fast → fast
hard → hard
late → late
Be careful: hard means with effort; hardly means almost not. “He works hard” ≠ “He hardly works.” “Late” means after the expected time; “lately” means recently.

Common confusing pairs

She sings well. (not “good” in this meaning)
He runs fast. (not “fastly”)
We arrived late. (not “lately” unless you mean recently)
I have hardly any money. (almost no money)

Where do adverbs go?

MannerOften after the verb/object: She spoke clearly. He completed the work carefully.
FrequencyUsually before a main verb: I often read. But after be: She is always kind.
TimeOften at the end or beginning: We met yesterday. Yesterday, we met.
PlaceOften after the verb/object: They waited outside.
DegreeBefore the word it modifies: very tall; quite slowly.
Negative meaningNever is usually before a main verb: I never smoke.
Position can change with emphasis and sentence structure. These are useful common patterns, not rules without exceptions.

Frequency ladder

Always100% — I always brush my teeth.
Usuallymost of the time — We usually eat at home.
Oftenmany times — She often calls.
Sometimeson some occasions — I sometimes cook.
Rarely / Seldomnot often — He rarely complains.
Never0% — They never arrive late.

Adverb highlighter

Choose a sentence and identify the adverb.

Common mistakes

Wrong: She sings beautiful. → Correct: She sings beautifully.
Wrong: He runs fastly. → Correct: He runs fast.
Wrong: I often am tired. → More natural: I am often tired.
Wrong: He works hardly. (if you mean with effort) → Correct: He works hard.
Wrong: She did the task careful. → Correct: She did the task carefully.
