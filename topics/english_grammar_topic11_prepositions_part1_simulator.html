Prepositions — Part 1

Learn the basic words that show place, time, and movement: in, on, at, under, between, into, onto, through, and more. Use visual examples and practise with 40 questions.

What is a preposition?

A preposition shows a relationship between a noun/pronoun and another part of a sentence. It often tells us where, when, or in which direction.

PlaceThe keys are under the book.
TimeWe meet at 6 pm.
MovementShe walked into the room.
A preposition is often followed by a noun or pronoun: on the table, after lunch, with him. That group is called a prepositional phrase.

Place, time, movement: quick map

Placein, on, at, under, over, behind, beside, between, near, in front of
Timeat, on, in, before, after, during, until, since, for
Movementto, into, onto, from, through, across, along, past, out of

Prepositions of place

IN — insideThe toy is in the box.
ON — touching a surfaceThe phone is on the desk.
AT — a point or locationMeet me at the gate.
UNDER — belowThe cat is under the chair.
ABOVE / OVER — higher thanThe lamp hangs above the table. The plane flew over the city.
BEHIND — at the backThe garden is behind the house.
IN FRONT OF — before something spatiallyThe car is in front of the shop.
BESIDE / NEXT TO — at the sideSit beside me.
BETWEEN — in the middle of twoThe bank is between the café and the library.
AMONG — within a groupShe stood among her friends.
NEAR — not far awayWe live near the station.
OPPOSITE — facingThe school is opposite the park.
Easy memory: in = inside; on = surface; at = a point or specific location. Usage depends on context, especially for addresses and places.

Prepositions of time: at, on, in

AT — exact timeat 7:30; at noon; at midnight; at night
ON — days and dateson Monday; on 12 June; on my birthday
IN — longer periodsin July; in 2026; in winter; in the morning
Compare: at 8 o'clock, on Friday, in October. We usually say “at night” but “in the morning/afternoon/evening.”

Other time prepositions

BeforeWash your hands before dinner.
AfterWe went home after class.
DuringPlease be quiet during the exam.
UntilI waited until 5 pm.
SinceI have lived here since 2020. (starting point)
ForI have lived here for six years. (duration)
Since names when something began; for tells how long it has continued. “Since Monday” vs “for three days.”

Prepositions of movement

TO — destinationWe went to school.
INTO — enterShe walked into the room.
ONTO — move to a surfaceThe cat jumped onto the bed.
FROM — starting pointHe came from Jaipur.
THROUGH — enter and exit an interiorWe drove through a tunnel.
ACROSS — from one side to anotherThey walked across the road.
ALONG — following a line/pathWe walked along the river.
PAST — beyond a pointShe ran past the shop.
OUT OF — exitHe stepped out of the car.
UP / DOWNThey climbed up the hill and walked down the stairs.
Compare: “The child is in the room” (location) vs “The child walked into the room” (movement to inside).

Common mistakes

Wrong: I wake up in 7 am. → Correct: I wake up at 7 am.
Wrong: My birthday is at Monday. → Correct: My birthday is on Monday.
Wrong: She walked in the room. (if emphasizing entering) → Better: She walked into the room.
Wrong: He has worked here since five years. → Correct: He has worked here for five years.
Wrong: The cat is between the furniture. (if within a group) → Often: The cat is among the furniture.
