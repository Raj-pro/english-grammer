Speaking Fluency & Everyday Conversations

Practise natural responses, follow-up questions, polite requests, and everyday conversation. Aim to communicate clearly—not to speak perfectly or as fast as possible.

1. The simple conversation pattern

Use this pattern when you are unsure what to say next:

React
“That sounds interesting!”
Add a detail
“I enjoy that kind of music too.”
Ask a follow-up
“Who is your favourite singer?”

You do not need to use all three steps every time. Choose what feels natural.

2. Useful conversation chunks

Thinking time

“Let me think for a moment.”
“That’s an interesting question.”

Clarifying

“Could you explain what you mean?”
“Could you say that again, please?”

Giving an opinion

“I think…”
“In my experience…”
“I see your point, but…”

Repairing speech

“What I mean is…”
“Let me rephrase that.”
“Sorry, I meant Thursday.”

Polite requests

“Could you help me with this?”
“Would you mind explaining it?”

Ending a chat

“It was nice talking to you.”
“I’ll see you later!”

3. Speak in meaningful chunks

Do not try to say every word separately. Pause briefly between ideas.

“Yesterday, / I went to the library / because I needed a quiet place to study.”

Read it aloud twice: first slowly, then at a comfortable conversational pace. Keep the pauses at the slashes.

4. What to do when you forget a word

  1. Describe the thing: “It’s a tool you use to open a bottle.”
  2. Give an example: “It’s like the app we used last week.”
  3. Use simpler words and continue.

Remember: fluency means keeping communication moving. It does not mean never making mistakes.

5. Mini model conversation

A: How are you finding the new course?
B: I’m enjoying it. The lessons are practical, but the assignments take time.
A: I can understand that. Which lesson has been the most useful so far?
B: The speaking activities, because they help me practise in real situations.

A 10-minute daily routine

Minutes 1–2 · Listen

Listen to a short dialogue or read a model aloud.

Minutes 3–4 · Shadow

Repeat the phrases, copying rhythm and pauses.

Minutes 5–7 · Personalise

Change the names, details, and situation to match your life.

Minutes 8–10 · Extend

Answer one extra question and ask a follow-up question.

Weekly goal: practise one everyday situation each day. Focus on one improvement at a time, such as clearer pauses or fewer repeated fillers.

Quick speaking challenge

Speak for 30–45 seconds about this topic:

“Describe something you learned recently and explain how it helped you.”

Try this structure: What it was → how you learned it → why it helped → one example.

If you use text-to-speech, listen for clarity—not whether the voice sounds exactly like you.
