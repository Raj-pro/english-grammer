Common Grammar Errors

Spot the mistakes that English learners often make and learn how to correct them. Practise agreement, tense, articles, prepositions, pronouns, word order, and commonly confused words.

1. How to correct grammar errors

When a sentence sounds wrong, do not change random words. Check it systematically.

1. Find the subject

Who or what is the sentence about?

2. Check the verb

Does it agree with the subject? Is the tense appropriate?

3. Check the noun

Does it need an article, plural ending, or determiner?

4. Check relationships

Look at prepositions, pronouns, word order, and punctuation.

Common mistakes at a glance

IncorrectCorrectWhy?
She go to work.She goes to work.Third-person singular in the simple present.
They is ready.They are ready.Plural subject–verb agreement.
I am living here since 2020.I have lived here since 2020.Present perfect for a situation continuing from the past.

Important note

Some English patterns vary by region, formality, and context. This lesson focuses on widely accepted standard English, especially for exams, formal writing, and everyday communication.

2. Subject–verb agreement errors

  • He/she/it + -s in simple present: She works; he studies; it rains.
  • I am, you/we/they are, he/she/it is.
  • Each/every + singular noun + singular verb: Every student has a book.
  • With there is/are, the verb agrees with the noun that follows: There are two files; there is one file.
  • Do not let a nearby noun distract you: “The list of items is long.” The subject is list.
Incorrect: The quality of these products are excellent.
Correct: The quality of these products is excellent.

Tense and verb-form errors

Common errorBetter formReason
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.A finished past time such as yesterday normally takes simple past.
She didn't went.She didn't go.After did/didn't, use the base verb.
He has went home.He has gone home.Present perfect = has/have + past participle.
I am agree.I agree.Agree is a verb; it does not need “am.”
She can sings.She can sing.Modal + base verb.
I look forward to meet you.I look forward to meeting you.Here “to” is a preposition and takes an -ing form.

Use than with many comparative forms: “This route is shorter than that one.” Use as + adjective + as for equality: “as fast as.”

3. Articles and countability

  • Use a/an with a singular countable noun when it is not specifically identified: a book, an apple.
  • Use the when the listener can identify which one: the book on the desk.
  • Do not usually put a/an directly before uncountable nouns: advice, information, furniture, equipment.
  • Use plural nouns for general countable categories: “Dogs are friendly” (general statement).
Incorrect: “She is engineer.” Correct: “She is an engineer.”
Incorrect: “I need an information.” Correct: “I need some information” or “a piece of information.”

4. Sentence structure mistakes

Fragment

Because I was tired.

I went to bed because I was tired.

A dependent clause alone is usually incomplete.

Run-on

I was tired I went to bed.

I was tired, so I went to bed.

Join independent clauses correctly.

Comma splice

It was late, we left.

It was late, so we left.

A comma alone usually cannot join two independent clauses.

Double negative

I don't know nothing.

I don't know anything.

In standard formal English, avoid unintended double negatives.

Word order and parallel structure

  • Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb: “I usually drink tea,” but after be: “I am usually early.”
  • Use parallel forms in lists: “She likes reading, swimming, and cycling.”
  • Keep pronouns clear: avoid “When Rahul met Amit, he was late” if “he” is ambiguous.
  • Use adjective + enough (“strong enough”) but enough + noun (“enough strength”).
  • Use “because” for a reason or “so” for a result; avoid awkwardly joining both in one clause.
Incorrect: “She enjoys to read, swimming, and to cycle.”
Correct: “She enjoys reading, swimming, and cycling.”

Proofreading checklist

  1. Underline the subject and circle the main verb.
  2. Check tense markers: yesterday, since, already, tomorrow.
  3. Check countable/uncountable nouns and articles.
  4. Check pronouns and possessives.
  5. Read preposition phrases aloud.
  6. Check that every sentence is complete and clauses are joined correctly.
