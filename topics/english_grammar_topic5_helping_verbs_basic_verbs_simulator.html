Helping Verbs & Basic Verbs

Learn am, is, are, was, were, has, have, had, do, does, did—and discover when a verb is the main verb or a helping verb. Read the lesson, try the mini activities, then complete 35 questions.

What is a verb?

A verb tells us an action, a state, or what someone/something is. Every complete English sentence normally needs a verb.

ActionRiya runs every morning.
State or feelingI know the answer.
BeingThey are happy.

Main verb vs helping verb

The main verb gives the central meaning. A helping verb (auxiliary) works with a main verb to make a tense, question, negative, or another verb form.

Helping She is reading a book.
is helps; reading tells the action.
Main verb She is tired.
Here, is links “she” with the state “tired”; it is the main verb, not a helping verb.
Important: The same word can be a main verb in one sentence and a helping verb in another. Look at how it is used.

Quick reference chart

AM / IS / AREI am; he/she/it is; you/we/they are.
WAS / WEREI/he/she/it was; you/we/they were.
HAVE / HAS / HADI/you/we/they have; he/she/it has; had works with all subjects in the past.
DO / DOES / DIDI/you/we/they do; he/she/it does; did works with all subjects in the past.
After does or did in a question or negative, use the base verb: “Does she like tea?” and “He did not go.” Not “likes” or “went” after does/did.

Be verbs: am, is, are, was, were

Use present forms for now or general facts. Use past forms for an earlier time.

AMI am ready.
ISHe is kind. / It is cold.
AREYou are early. / We are friends.
WASShe was tired yesterday.
WEREThey were at home.

Three common patterns

Be + adjective/noun/place: He is happy. We are students. I was at school.

Be + verb-ing: He is running. They were studying. Here, be is helping.

Be + past participle: The window was broken by the ball. This is a passive structure (learn more in the voice topic).

Remember: “You were” is correct for one person or many people when speaking to “you.”

Have, has, had

These words can show possession or experience. They can also help another verb form a perfect tense.

HAVEI have two pens.
HASMaya has a bicycle.
HADWe had a small garden.
Helping HAVEShe has finished her work.
Helping HADThey had left before noon.
Present: he/she/it has; I/you/we/they have. Past: all subjects use had. When have/has/had is followed by a past participle like finished, eaten, seen, it is often helping.

Do, does, did

These words can mean “perform” as main verbs, or help form questions and negatives.

DO (main)I do my chores.
DO (helping)Do you speak English?
DOES (helping)Does he live here?
DID (helping)Did they call you?
NegativeShe does not eat meat.
Past negativeWe did not see it.
After do/does/did as helping verbs, use the base form of the main verb: “Does he work?” “Did she come?” “They did not play.”

Common mistakes

Incorrect: She are happy. → Correct: She is happy.
Incorrect: He have a car. → Correct: He has a car.
Incorrect: Does she likes tea? → Correct: Does she like tea?
Incorrect: They did not went. → Correct: They did not go.
Incorrect: I am agree. → Correct: I agree.
