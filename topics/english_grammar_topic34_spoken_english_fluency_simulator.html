Topic 34: Spoken English Fluency

Build smoother conversations with useful phrases, follow-up questions, and speaking drills.

1. The conversation loop

Listen

“Right, I see.”

Show attention without interrupting.

React

“That sounds exciting!”

Respond to the meaning or feeling.

Follow up

“What happened next?”

Ask an open question to continue.

Share

“That reminds me of…”

Add a related thought, then return the conversation.

2. Phrases that give you thinking time

“That's an interesting question.” — acknowledge the question.

“Let me think about that for a moment.” — pause naturally.

“I'd say…” — introduce an opinion.

“I might be wrong, but…” — express uncertainty politely.

“Let me rephrase that.” — correct or clarify what you said.

3. A simple storytelling structure

Context: “Last weekend, I went to…”
Event: “At first…, but then…”
Result: “In the end…”
Reaction: “The best part was…”

Use past tense for completed events, sequence words to guide the listener, and a few relevant details.

4. Daily 10-minute routine

  1. Read a model phrase aloud twice.
  2. Speak for one minute about your day.
  3. Ask yourself two follow-up questions.
  4. Record yourself if possible; listen for unclear sentences.
  5. Repeat, making the message clearer—not necessarily faster.

Speaking Drills

Read the model aloud, then make your own version.

Tell a short story

Speak or write for 45–60 seconds.

Start: “Last week, something unexpected happened…”
Then: “At first… After that…”
End: “In the end… What I learned was…”
