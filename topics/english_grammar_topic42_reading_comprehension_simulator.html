Reading Comprehension

Understand passages, identify main ideas, infer meaning, and answer questions using evidence.

1. Main idea and supporting details

The main idea is what a paragraph is mostly about. Supporting details explain, prove, or illustrate that idea.

Example: “Walking is an easy way to add movement to the day. It needs little equipment, can fit into short breaks, and may help people feel more energetic.”
Main idea: Walking is a simple way to add physical activity.
Details: It needs little equipment and fits into short breaks.

2. Skimming versus scanning

Skimming

Read quickly to understand the overall topic and structure. Look at the title, headings, first sentences, and conclusion.

Scanning

Search for one specific item, such as a date, name, number, or keyword.

“What year did the project begin?” → Scan for a year.
“What is the passage mainly about?” → Skim first, then confirm.

3. Inference and evidence

An inference is a logical conclusion based on clues in the passage, not a random guess.

Text: “Arun checked the dark sky, picked up an umbrella, and hurried outside.”
Inference: Arun may expect rain.
Evidence: The dark sky and the umbrella.

4. Context clues and signal words

  • Contrast: however, although, whereas
  • Cause: because, since
  • Result: therefore, consequently
  • Example: for instance, for example
  • Addition: also, in addition

For unfamiliar words, use surrounding sentences and word parts before checking a dictionary.

Reading Lab: read, think, answer

Read the passage and check your understanding.

Passage A: The Community Garden

Last spring, residents of an apartment building turned an unused patch of land into a community garden. Each household could grow herbs or vegetables in a small shared plot. At first, some residents worried that the garden would require too much time. The organizers responded by creating a weekly rota, so no one had to care for the whole garden alone.

By the end of the summer, the garden was producing tomatoes, mint, and beans. Neighbours who had rarely spoken began exchanging gardening advice and sharing extra produce. The organizers noticed that the garden had become more than a place to grow food: it had also become a place for people to meet. They now plan to add a compost bin and invite more residents to join.

Question 1: What is the main idea?

Question 2: Why did organizers create a weekly rota?

Question 3: What can you infer about the residents?

Question 4: Write a one-sentence summary

A reliable method for reading questions

  1. Preview: read the title and notice the structure.
  2. Read for gist: ask what the whole passage is mostly about.
  3. Read the question carefully: identify whether it asks for a fact, main idea, inference, or word meaning.
  4. Find evidence: return to the relevant sentence or paragraph.
  5. Eliminate weak choices: remove answers contradicted by the passage or unsupported by it.
  6. Answer directly: use the information needed to answer well.
Remember: An answer can sound reasonable and still be wrong if the passage does not support it.
