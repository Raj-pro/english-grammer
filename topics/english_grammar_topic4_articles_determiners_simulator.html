📖 Easy rules and examples

A / An

Use a or an with a singular countable noun when it is not specific. Choose by sound: a university (starts with /y/ sound), an hour (silent h).

The

Use the when the listener knows which one, when it was mentioned already, or with unique things in context: I saw a dog. The dog was friendly.

No article

Often use no article for general plural nouns and uncountable nouns: Cats like sleep. Water is essential. But use articles when talking about a specific one: The water in this bottle is cold.

This / That / These / Those

This = one nearby; that = one farther away; these = multiple nearby; those = multiple farther away.

Some / Any

Some is common in affirmative sentences and offers/requests; any is common in questions and negatives. Usage can vary by meaning: Would you like some tea?

Much / Many / Few / Little

Many / few with plural countable nouns: many books, few chairs. Much / little with uncountable nouns: much time, little milk. A few = some; few = almost none. A little = some; little = almost none.

Each / Every / All / Both

Each/every focus on members one by one and usually take a singular noun: each student, every day. Both refers to two together. All refers to the whole group or amount.
